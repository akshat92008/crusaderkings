15

**Objective:** Make every major government type feel like a fundamentally different game.

Currently, Crown Monarch has multiple government profiles, but many share similar underlying mechanics. That must change.

### **1.1 European feudal governments**

Develop hereditary privileges, noble assemblies, royal charters, estate rights, aristocratic influence, obligations and negotiations over government reforms.

### **1.2 Indian kingdoms**

Develop a distinct political framework featuring regional chiefs, land grants, subordinate courts, temple patronage, royal assemblies, tributary relationships, competing dynasties and regional power struggles.

Indian kingdoms must not simply be European feudal kingdoms with different names.

### **1.3 Chinese imperial government**

Implement:

- Imperial bureaucracy.
- Civil service examinations.
- Official ranks and promotions.
- Provincial governors.
- Merit and political influence.
- Court factions.
- Imperial legitimacy.
- Administrative corruption.
- Dynastic decline and restoration.

### **1.4 Japanese shogunate**

Develop competing military houses, imperial recognition, shogunal authority, regional lords, political loyalty, house rivalries and struggles for national dominance.

### **1.5 Islamic sultanates**

Develop military patronage, religious legitimacy, court factions, appointed governors, scholarly institutions, succession disputes and competing military elites.

### **1.6 Steppe and nomadic governments**

Introduce clan influence, herds, migration incentives, tribal assemblies, confederations, leadership challenges and seasonal political pressures.

### **1.7 Merchant republics**

Develop merchant families, commercial influence, political elections, family prestige, commercial rivalries, political appointments and oligarchic competition.

Codex will handle the underlying economy; my work will focus on the political and institutional gameplay built upon it.

### **1.8 Additional political structures**

Extend tributary states, African kingdoms, confederations, centralized monarchies, city-states and Indigenous political systems using historically appropriate distinctions.

### **Completion criteria**

Every government must have unique mechanics, resources, decisions, events, political threats, AI priorities and dedicated UI information.

Changing government should fundamentally alter the player's strategy.

### **4.1 Scheme categories**

| Personal schemes | Political schemes       | Hostile schemes    |
| ---------------- | ----------------------- | ------------------ |
| Befriend         | Influence councillor    | Assassination      |
| Sway             | Fabricate evidence      | Abduction          |
| Courtship        | Undermine authority     | Discredit          |
| Patronage        | Influence succession    | Expose secrets     |
| Reconciliation   | Recruit faction members | Political sabotage |

### **4.2 Scheme mechanics**

Every scheme must have preparation time, participants, agent recruitment, discovery risk, success probability, opposing counterintelligence, resource requirements and multiple possible outcomes.

### **4.3 Intelligence networks**

Create persistent intelligence relationships involving informants, spies, courtiers and political agents.

Agents should participate because of loyalty, wealth, fear, personal relationships, ambition or political interests.

### **4.4 Secrets and hooks**

Expand secrets, evidence, blackmail, political leverage and exposure consequences.

A discovered secret should produce actionable political opportunities rather than just appear in a list.

### **4.5 Autonomous intrigue**

AI characters must independently develop rivalries, recruit agents, plot against opponents, protect themselves and react to discovered conspiracies.

The player should not be the only person initiating schemes.
