# Historical context — 1450

All 56 catalogued starting profiles now have explanatory references. These are paraphrases, not a verified demographic, genealogical or territorial reconstruction. Composite regional profiles are explicitly marked. These entries do not alter simulation state or atlas ownership. Source links support historical background; precise starting rulers, houses, population, faith, and district boundaries require separate validation.

## England in 1450 (`england`)

Henry VI's claim to France was increasingly difficult to sustain as the Hundred Years' War approached its end.

Source: [Royal Armouries — The Hundred Years' War](https://royalarmouries.org/objects-and-stories/stories/the-hundred-years-war-1337-1453).

## France in 1450 (`france`)

Charles VII's recovery of French territory transformed the closing years of the Hundred Years' War. The campaign does not force the historical outcome.

Source: [Royal Armouries — The Hundred Years' War](https://royalarmouries.org/objects-and-stories/stories/the-hundred-years-war-1337-1453).

## Vijayanagara in the mid-fifteenth century (`vijayanagara`)

The southern empire remained a major regional power after Deva Raya II's death. Relations with Deccan courts were shaped by both conflict and alliances.

Source: [Metropolitan Museum of Art — South Asia chronology](https://www.metmuseum.org/toah/ht/08/ssa.html).

## The Bahmani Sultanate (`bahmani`)

Founded in 1347, the sultanate was still a major Deccan polity in 1450; its later division into successor states had not yet occurred.

Source: [Metropolitan Museum of Art — South Asia chronology](https://www.metmuseum.org/toah/ht/08/ssa.html).

## Delhi at a dynastic transition (`delhi`)

The Sayyid dynasty was nearing the end of its rule; the Lodi dynasty succeeded it in 1451. This transition is historical context, not a forced game event.

Source: [Metropolitan Museum of Art — South Asia chronology](https://www.metmuseum.org/toah/ht/08/ssa.html).

## Ming after the Tumu crisis (`ming`)

The capture of the Zhengtong emperor in 1449 led to the accession of his half-brother, the Jingtai emperor. The political crisis influenced military and court institutions.

Source: [UC Berkeley — Taming the Army](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/4p79j55k).

## Joseon and Ming diplomacy (`joseon`)

The court navigated a period of political change in China following the 1449 crisis while maintaining its diplomatic relationship with Ming.

Source: [Korea Citation Index — Jingtai reign and Joseon diplomacy](https://www.kci.go.kr/kciportal/landing/article.kci?arti_id=ART003003444).

## Mamluk political institutions (`mamluks`)

The Burji period involved a military elite with forms of advancement and succession that were not exclusively hereditary. Cairo and eastern Mediterranean cities benefited from trade and patronage.

Source: [Metropolitan Museum of Art — The Art of the Mamluk Period](https://www.metmuseum.org/pt/essays/the-art-of-the-mamluk-period-1250-1517).

## The Triple Alliance in the fifteenth century (`mexica`)

Expansion across the Basin of Mexico and urban development at Tenochtitlan formed part of a changing regional political landscape. Later imperial extent is not assumed for 1450.

Source: [Metropolitan Museum of Art — Mexico chronology](https://artistproject.metmuseum.org/toah/ht/08/canm.html).

## Andean consolidation (`inca`)

Inca expansion from the Cuzco region was underway during the fifteenth century. The empire's much larger later extent should not be read back into the starting year.

Source: [Metropolitan Museum of Art — Central and Southern Andes](https://82nd-and-fifth.metmuseum.org/toah/ht/08/sanc.html).

## Maya political diversity (`maya`)

**Composite regional profile.** The region contained multiple political communities, including the expanding K’iche’ kingdom in the Guatemalan highlands. The single playable profile is a gameplay abstraction.

Source: [Metropolitan Museum of Art — Maya Area chronology](https://artistproject.metmuseum.org/toah/ht/08/cana.html).

## Castile and the Iberian balance (`castile`)

Castile remained a distinct crown in 1450. The later dynastic union with Aragon and conquest of Granada were not yet accomplished.

Source: [Met — Iberian Peninsula chronology](https://artistproject.metmuseum.org/toah/ht/08/eusi.html).

## Portugal and the Atlantic (`portugal`)

Portugal maintained a separate monarchy on the Iberian Peninsula. Maritime expeditions increasingly connected its court and merchants to Atlantic routes; later overseas empire is not starting territory.

Source: [Met — Europe and the Age of Exploration](https://www.metmuseum.org/de/essays/europe-and-the-age-of-exploration).

## The Crown of Aragon (`aragon`)

Aragon was a composite crown with Mediterranean interests and distinct territorial institutions. Its later union with Castile is not assumed at campaign start.

Source: [Met — Iberian Peninsula chronology](https://artistproject.metmuseum.org/toah/ht/08/eusi.html).

## The Burgundian court (`burgundy`)

Philip the Good's rule linked territories in Burgundy and the Low Countries. Court patronage and urban wealth supported a politically complex collection of lands.

Source: [Met — Western Europe chronology](https://artistproject.metmuseum.org/toah/ht/08/euwl.html).

## The imperial estates (`imperial`)

**Composite regional profile.** The Holy Roman Empire contained numerous princes, ecclesiastical territories and cities, with authority distributed among them. The playable imperial profile aggregates these distinct jurisdictions rather than representing a unitary state.

Source: [Met — Central Europe chronology](https://www.metmuseum.org/ko/toah/ht/08/euwc).

## Poland and Lithuania (`poland`)

The dynastic association between Poland and Lithuania dated from the late fourteenth century. Their institutions and territorial interests remained distinct; the later Commonwealth is not the starting polity.

Source: [Met — Eastern Europe chronology](https://www.metmuseum.org/toah/ht/08/eue.html).

## Muscovite succession conflict (`muscovy`)

Vasily II's reign was marked by a prolonged conflict over the grand princely succession. The campaign permits different outcomes rather than starting with the centralized state of later centuries.

Source: [EBSCO — Russian Civil War, 1425–1462](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/history/russian-civil-war-1425-1462/).

## Novgorod's autonomy (`novgorod`)

Novgorod remained a separate political community in 1450, navigating rivalry among Muscovite claimants and relationships with Lithuania. Its later incorporation into Moscow is not predetermined.

Source: [Springer — Novgorod and the Muscovite dynastic conflict](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1134/S1019331622110090).

## Ottoman power before 1453 (`ottomans`)

Murad II's Ottoman polity was a major power in southeastern Europe and Anatolia. Constantinople remained outside Ottoman rule at the beginning of 1450.

Source: [Met — Western Asia chronology](https://www.metmuseum.org/fr/toah/ht/08/waa).

## Timurid succession and regional courts (`timurids`)

**Composite regional profile.** After Shah Rukh's death in 1447, competing Timurid interests reshaped authority across the region. The game profile simplifies a divided political landscape.

Source: [Encyclopaedia Iranica — Aq Qoyunlu](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/aq-qoyunlu-confederation/).

## Qara Qoyunlu and regional rivalry (`qara`)

The Turkmen confederations competed for influence in western Iran and adjacent lands as Timurid power fragmented. Confederated authority should not be mistaken for uniform provincial control.

Source: [Encyclopaedia Iranica — Aq Qoyunlu](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/aq-qoyunlu-confederation/).

## The western steppe (`horde`)

**Composite regional profile.** The Golden Horde's political inheritance was fragmenting into competing centers and successor khanates. The campaign's single profile simplifies that changing distribution of power.

Source: [Cambridge History of Inner Asia — Western Steppe](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/cambridge-history-of-inner-asia/western-steppe-volgaural-region-siberia-and-the-crimea/DB26B77ED4D3AB3405F62FE800BC1F96).

## Marinid Morocco (`morocco`)

The Marinid dynasty remained in power under Abd al-Haqq II. Its later replacement and the political developments of subsequent decades are not assumed at the starting date.

Source: [Archnet — Marinid dynasty](https://www.archnet.org/collections/2366).

## The Ethiopian highland kingdom (`ethiopia`)

The Solomonic dynasty governed a Christian kingdom in the highlands, with religious institutions and regional relationships important to political authority.

Source: [Met — Africa chronology](https://www.metmuseum.org/ko/toah/ht/08/afa.html).

## Mali and the western Sudan (`mali`)

Mali retained a political and commercial legacy across the western Sudan as its authority weakened and other regional powers gained influence. Its earlier maximum extent is not treated as 1450 ownership.

Source: [Met — Western Sudan chronology](https://82nd-and-fifth.metmuseum.org/toah/ht/08/afu.html).

## Songhai before imperial expansion (`songhai`)

Songhai was a rising power around Gao and the Niger. The larger empire associated with the subsequent reign of Sunni Ali does not yet exist at campaign start.

Source: [Met — Western Sudan chronology](https://82nd-and-fifth.metmuseum.org/toah/ht/08/afu.html).

## The Kingdom of Kongo (`kongo`)

Central African political networks included the Kingdom of Kongo, whose regional authority and trade preceded the later arrival of Portuguese contacts.

Source: [Met — Central Africa chronology](https://www.metmuseum.org/es/toah/ht/08/afc.html).

## Kilwa and the Swahili coast (`kilwa`)

Kilwa belonged to the maritime trading communities of the East African coast, linked to the Indian Ocean commercial network. Coastal cities were not a single centralized political territory.

Source: [Met — Africa chronology](https://www.metmuseum.org/ko/toah/ht/08/afa.html).

## The independent Bengal Sultanate (`bengal`)

Bengal was governed by the restored Ilyas Shahi dynasty during the mid-fifteenth century. It maintained its own political identity distinct from Delhi.

Source: [Later Ilyas Shahi Rulers — historical study](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/348317336_LATER_ILYAS_SHAHI_RULERS).

## The Gujarat Sultanate (`gujarat`)

Gujarat's independent sultanate emerged in the early fifteenth century, with a court at Ahmedabad and connections to commercial networks. Regional control was not identical to modern state borders.

Source: [Cambridge — In Praise of Kings: Contextualising Fifteenth-Century Gujarat](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/in-praise-of-kings/setting-the-stage-contextualising-fifteenthcentury-gujarat/C1FFDF6F6993271D2D1BD09425DBDF30).

## Muromachi Japan (`ashikaga`)

Ashikaga shogunal authority coexisted with powerful regional warrior houses. The later Onin War and Sengoku fragmentation are possible historical developments, not predetermined conditions.

Source: [Met — Japan chronology](https://www.metmuseum.org/ko/toah/ht/08/eaj).

## Ayutthaya's regional networks (`ayutthaya`)

Ayutthaya, founded in 1350, developed into a major political and trading center. Its riverine setting connected the court to regional and international commerce.

Source: [UNESCO — Historic City of Ayutthaya](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/576).

## The restored Le court (`dai-viet`)

The Le dynasty restored Vietnamese political independence following Ming occupation in the early fifteenth century. Court administration and regional trade developed within this changing landscape.

Source: [Met — Southeast Asia chronology](https://artistproject.metmuseum.org/toah/ht/08/sse.html).

## Majapahit and the Indonesian archipelago (`majapahit`)

Majapahit remained part of a complex maritime political landscape in the fifteenth century. The campaign does not equate earlier claims of influence with direct ownership of all islands.

Source: [Met — Southeast Asia chronology](https://artistproject.metmuseum.org/toah/ht/08/sse.html).

## Malacca's trading port (`malacca`)

Malacca emerged around the beginning of the fifteenth century as an important entrepot on the strait, connecting regional maritime markets.

Source: [Met — Southeast Asia chronology](https://artistproject.metmuseum.org/toah/ht/08/sse.html).

## Pueblo communities (`pueblo`)

**Composite regional profile.** Southwestern North America contained diverse settled communities with distinctive village organization and architecture. The playable profile aggregates communities; it is not a single historical kingdom.

Source: [Met — North America chronology](https://82nd-and-fifth.metmuseum.org/toah/ht/08/na.html).

## Haudenosaunee political tradition (`haudenosaunee`)

**Composite regional profile.** The Haudenosaunee Confederacy's own traditions describe governance through the Great Law of Peace. No single disputed foundation date is imposed by this campaign.

Source: [Haudenosaunee Confederacy — Who We Are](https://www.haudenosauneeconfederacy.com/who-we-are/).

## Muisca highland communities (`muisca`)

**Composite regional profile.** The Colombian highlands supported multiple Muisca political and ceremonial centers. The single game profile is a regional abstraction, not a unified nation-state.

Source: [Met — Northern Andes chronology](https://www.metmuseum.org/es/toah/ht/08/sann.html).

## Tupi-speaking communities (`tupi`)

**Composite regional profile.** Tupi-speaking peoples inhabited diverse regions and maintained distinct communities. A linguistic grouping is not equivalent to one sovereign polity; the game profile is deliberately composite.

Source: [Population history of Tupi-speaking peoples — research article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11774006/).

## Mapuche regional communities (`mapuche`)

**Composite regional profile.** Mapuche communities developed distinctive regional social and cultural traditions. The profile represents multiple communities rather than a centralized kingdom with a single hereditary ruler.

Source: [Museo Chileno de Arte Precolombino — Mapuche](https://museo.precolombino.cl/aprende/pueblos-culturas/pueblos-originarios/mapuche/).

## Māori communities in Aotearoa (`maori`)

**Composite regional profile.** Polynesian settlement of Aotearoa preceded 1450. Its communities developed distinct local identities and relationships; the playable profile does not represent a single national polity.

Source: [Te Ara — When was New Zealand first settled?](https://teara.govt.nz/en/when-was-new-zealand-first-settled/print).

## Fijian political communities (`fiji`)

**Composite regional profile.** Fijian societies developed regional forms of leadership, settlement and competition. The profile combines multiple political communities and does not assert a single kingdom in 1450.

Source: [Oxford Handbook — Fijian archaeology](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/34645/chapter-abstract/295209329).

## First Peoples of Australia (`aboriginal`)

**Composite regional profile.** Australia was home to many distinct Aboriginal peoples, languages, countries and systems of law. The playable regional aggregation is not a historical sovereign state, nor does the map imply unoccupied land.

Source: [AIATSIS — First Peoples of Australia](https://aiatsis.gov.au/explore/first-peoples-australia).

## Scotland under James II (`scotland`)

James II's court faced the political power of the Douglas family and other nobles. Border conflict with England and internal rivalries shaped the period.

Source: [Historic Environment Scotland — Battle of Sark](https://portal.historicenvironment.scot/apex/f?p=1505%3A300%3A%3A%3A%3A%3AVIEWTYPE%2CVIEWREF%3Adesignation%2CBTL40).

## The Breton principality (`brittany`)

The Montfort dukes strengthened their own political institutions in the context of English and French rivalry. Brittany was not yet incorporated into the French kingdom.

Source: [Nantes History Museum — Castle of the Dukes of Brittany](https://artsandculture.google.com/story/the-castle-of-the-dukes-of-brittany-ch%C3%A2teau-des-ducs-de-bretagne/HAXR0rCdCTt3LA?hl=en).

## The contested Kalmar Union (`kalmar`)

**Composite regional profile.** The Nordic crowns shared a dynastic union whose authority was contested following Christopher's death. The playable union profile aggregates distinct kingdoms and does not imply uniform administration.

Source: [Urban History — The Kalmar Union, 1397–1523](https://stadshistoria.gamlahalmstad.se/en/stadshistoria-inlagg/the-kalmar-union-1397-1523-en/).

## Genoese maritime networks (`genoa`)

The Republic of Genoa's merchants maintained trading settlements across the Mediterranean and Black Sea. Commercial reach was broader than its directly administered territories.

Source: [UNESCO Tentative List — Genoese Trading Posts](https://whc.unesco.org/en/tentativelists/5575).

## Venice and its maritime republic (`venice`)

Venice's political and commercial institutions connected the lagoon city to mainland and maritime territories. The profile represents a republic rather than a hereditary feudal kingdom.

Source: [Oxford Reference — Venice](https://academic.oup.com/reference/62416/reference-article/561733011?login=false).

## Lusignan Cyprus (`cyprus`)

The island kingdom preserved a Lusignan political inheritance and culturally diverse population within the eastern Mediterranean. Its later absorption by Venice was still in the future.

Source: [Cambridge History of the Crusades — Lusignan Cyprus](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/cambridge-history-of-the-crusades/lusignan-cyprus/BA514E0FE0D20B9C6A3ECA443E97DE04).

## The Four Oirats (`oirats`)

The Oirat confederation was a major steppe power during Esen's ascendancy, in the context of conflict and diplomacy with Ming. Its authority is represented through a simplified confederate profile.

Source: [Cambridge History of War — Legacy of the Nomadic Empires](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/cambridge-history-of-war/legacy-of-the-nomadic-empires/B614D7B187FB37BE7D778CE293802D99).

## Chagatai political inheritance (`chagatai`)

**Composite regional profile.** The Chagatai realm had fragmented long before 1450. The playable eastern steppe profile refers to the surviving regional inheritance, not the full territory of the original Mongol khanate.

Source: [OpenStax — The Mongol Empire Fragments](https://openstax.org/books/world-history-volume-1/pages/14-3-the-mongol-empire-fragments).

## Tibetan regional authority (`tibet`)

**Composite regional profile.** Fifteenth-century Tibet contained competing aristocratic and religious institutions. The campaign combines several regional interests and does not claim a unified government covering all Tibetan areas.

Source: [University of Hamburg — Buddhist Studies, Vol. 7](https://www.buddhismuskunde.uni-hamburg.de/pdf/4-publikationen/hamburg-buddhist-studies/hamburgup-hbs07-rheingans.pdf).

## Lan Xang's political networks (`lanxang`)

Lan Xang emerged in the fourteenth century within mainland Southeast Asia. Its relationships with regional centers should not be read as fixed modern national borders.

Source: [Wiley — Encyclopedia of Empire, Lan Xang](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/9781118455074.wbeoe154).

## The Sunda Kingdom (`sunda`)

The Sunda kingdom maintained a distinct political and cultural presence in western Java, separate from other Javanese centers during the fifteenth century.

Source: [Cambridge — Early Interactions Between South and Southeast Asia](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/early-interactions-between-south-and-southeast-asia/421F1ADADADBB5D38159BE2FCBB1DA8C/listing?pageNum=2).

## The Zayyanid court at Tlemcen (`zayyanid`)

Tlemcen was the center of the Zayyanid dynasty in the central Maghreb, with connections to Mediterranean and trans-Saharan networks. Its later political fate is not fixed.

Source: [Archnet — Zayyanid dynasty](https://next.archnet.org/collections/2376).
