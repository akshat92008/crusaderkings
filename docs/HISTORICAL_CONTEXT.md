# Historical context — 1450

This catalog adds explanatory references to the existing campaign without overriding Codex's simulation state or atlas polygons. The entries are paraphrases, not extracted source text. Historical reference, synthetic gameplay districts and later alternate history remain separate. Eleven playable profiles are covered in this pass; other profiles retain their existing information. Full historical ruler, house, population, faith and boundary validation is still outstanding.

## England in 1450 (`england`)

Henry VI's claim to France was increasingly difficult to sustain as the Hundred Years' War approached its end.

Source: [Royal Armouries — The Hundred Years' War](https://royalarmouries.org/objects-and-stories/stories/the-hundred-years-war-1337-1453).

## France in 1450 (`france`)

Charles VII's recovery of French territory transformed the closing years of the Hundred Years' War. The campaign does not force the historical outcome.

Source: [Royal Armouries — The Hundred Years' War](https://royalarmouries.org/objects-and-stories/stories/the-hundred-years-war-1337-1453).

## Vijayanagara in the mid-fifteenth century (`vijayanagara`)

The southern empire remained a major regional power after Deva Raya II's death. Relations with Deccan courts were shaped by both conflict and alliances.

Source: [Metropolitan Museum of Art — South Asia chronology](https://www.metmuseum.org/toah/ht/08/ssa.html).

## The Bahmani Sultanate (`bahmani`)

Founded in 1347, the sultanate was still a major Deccan polity in 1450; its later division into successor states had not yet occurred.

Source: [Metropolitan Museum of Art — South Asia chronology](https://www.metmuseum.org/toah/ht/08/ssa.html).

## Delhi at a dynastic transition (`delhi`)

The Sayyid dynasty was nearing the end of its rule; the Lodi dynasty succeeded it in 1451. This transition is historical context, not a forced game event.

Source: [Metropolitan Museum of Art — South Asia chronology](https://www.metmuseum.org/toah/ht/08/ssa.html).

## Ming after the Tumu crisis (`ming`)

The capture of the Zhengtong emperor in 1449 led to the accession of his half-brother, the Jingtai emperor. The political crisis influenced military and court institutions.

Source: [UC Berkeley — Taming the Army](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/4p79j55k).

## Joseon and Ming diplomacy (`joseon`)

The court navigated a period of political change in China following the 1449 crisis while maintaining its diplomatic relationship with Ming.

Source: [Korea Citation Index — Jingtai reign and Joseon diplomacy](https://www.kci.go.kr/kciportal/landing/article.kci?arti_id=ART003003444).

## Mamluk political institutions (`mamluks`)

The Burji period involved a military elite with forms of advancement and succession that were not exclusively hereditary. Cairo and eastern Mediterranean cities benefited from trade and patronage.

Source: [Metropolitan Museum of Art — The Art of the Mamluk Period](https://www.metmuseum.org/pt/essays/the-art-of-the-mamluk-period-1250-1517).

## The Triple Alliance in the fifteenth century (`mexica`)

Expansion across the Basin of Mexico and urban development at Tenochtitlan formed part of a changing regional political landscape. Later imperial extent is not assumed for 1450.

Source: [Metropolitan Museum of Art — Mexico chronology](https://artistproject.metmuseum.org/toah/ht/08/canm.html).

## Andean consolidation (`inca`)

Inca expansion from the Cuzco region was underway during the fifteenth century. The empire's much larger later extent should not be read back into the starting year.

Source: [Metropolitan Museum of Art — Central and Southern Andes](https://82nd-and-fifth.metmuseum.org/toah/ht/08/sanc.html).

## Maya political diversity (`maya`)

The region contained multiple political communities, including the expanding K’iche’ kingdom in the Guatemalan highlands. The single playable profile is a gameplay abstraction.

Source: [Metropolitan Museum of Art — Maya Area chronology](https://artistproject.metmuseum.org/toah/ht/08/cana.html).
